# Grade 8 Science: Force and Its Effects on Motion
## Task-Based Learning (TBL) Competency Decomposition

**Curricular Subject:** Science (Middle Stage, Grade 8)  
**Curriculum Document:** NCERT Grade 8 Science, Chapter 11 (*Force and Pressure*); NCF-SE 2023 Curricular Goal C-3.1 & C-3.2 (Science & Physical Phenomenon)  
**Competency Statement:** *"Understand the concept of force and its effects on motion and shape, distinguish contact and non-contact forces, and apply force principles to everyday physical interactions."*  
**Track Alignment:** Track A (Compulsory Core Curriculum)  
**Target Grade Band:** Middle Stage (Grade 8, Ages 13–14)  
**Multilingual Key Terms:**  
- **Force:** Hindi: बल (*Bal*); Marathi: बल (*Bal*); Kannada: ಬಲ (*Bala*); Tamil: விசை (*Visai*)  
- **Motion:** Hindi: गति (*Gati*); Marathi: गती (*Gati*); Kannada: ಚಲನೆ (*Chalane*); Tamil: இயக்கம் (*Iyakkam*)  
- **Friction:** Hindi: घर्षण (*Gharshan*); Marathi: घर्षण (*Gharshan*); Kannada: ಘರ್ಷಣೆ (*Gharshane*); Tamil: உராய்வு (*Uraaivu*)  
- **Gravity:** Hindi: गुरुत्वाकर्षण (*Gurutvaakarshan*); Marathi: गुरुत्वाकर्षण (*Gurutvaakarshan*); Kannada: ಗುರುತ್ವಾಕರ್ಷಣೆ (*Gurutvaakarshane*); Tamil: ஈர்ப்பு விசை (*Eerppu Visai*)  

---

## Sub-Competency Architecture

- **Sub-Competency 1 (SCI-8-FORCE-C1):** Define force as an interaction (push or pull) and classify types of forces into contact and non-contact categories.
- **Sub-Competency 2 (SCI-8-FORCE-C2):** Demonstrate and quantify how forces act in the same or opposite directions to produce a net resultant force.
- **Sub-Competency 3 (SCI-8-FORCE-C3):** Conduct classroom experiments to observe the effects of force on the speed, direction of motion, and shape of objects.
- **Sub-Competency 4 (SCI-8-FORCE-C4):** Analyze real-world friction and non-contact force phenomena (electrostatic, magnetic, gravitational) and their practical implications in safety and sports.

## Detailed Task Catalog

### Sub-Competency 1: Define Force and Classify Types (SCI-8-FORCE-C1)

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-1.1
- **Title:** Observe daily actions and classify them into push, pull, or dual interactions.
- **Time Estimate:** 20 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Paper, pencil, classroom objects)
- **Skill Type:** Conceptual Understanding
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Response / Observation Table
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Students inspect 6 routine school actions (opening a drawer, kicking a ball, drawing water from a well, hitting a shuttlecock, lifting a schoolbag, closing a door) and construct a 3-column verification matrix classifying each as Push, Pull, or Push+Pull simultaneously.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Correctly classifies all 6 observed actions into push, pull, or combination.
  2. Identifies that force requires an interaction between at least two objects.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a pre-filled hint card showing that "Drawing water from a well = Pull towards body; kicking football = Push away from foot."
- **Extension Challenge:** Identify 2 musical instruments where sound is produced by push vs. pull forces (e.g., tabla strike vs. sitar plucking).
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, Section 11.1–11.2, pp. 127–129; NCF-SE 2023 Sec 4.3.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Low resource requirement; works seamlessly in rural or urban classrooms without electricity.

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-1.2
- **Title:** Conduct magnet and comb tests to identify non-contact forces.
- **Time Estimate:** 30 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Bar magnet, paper clips, plastic ruler/comb, bits of dry paper)
- **Skill Type:** Procedural Skill & Observation
- **Assessment Mode:** Performance Task
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Rub a plastic comb through dry hair and hold it 2 cm above paper scraps without touching. Next, hold a bar magnet 2 cm away from an iron paper clip. Record whether physical contact is needed for motion to start.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Accurately demonstrates that movement begins before physical contact occurs.
  2. Correctly labels electrostatic force and magnetic force as action-at-a-distance (non-contact) forces.
- **Scaffold Support:** Pair students with a peer buddy and provide a diagram showing field lines of attraction without surface touch.
- **Extension Challenge:** Formulate an explanation for why gravitational force pulls an apple down without Earth physically touching it beforehand.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, Section 11.7, pp. 136–138.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Ensure paper scraps are cut into small pieces (under 5 mm) to guarantee visible static charge attraction.

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-1.3
- **Title:** Construct a Contact vs. Non-Contact Force Classification Tree.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Chart paper or student notebooks)
- **Skill Type:** Critical Thinking & Synthesis
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Diagram / Peer Review
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Create a two-branch hierarchical taxonomy diagram categorizing: Muscular force, Friction, Magnetic force, Electrostatic force, and Gravitational force, citing one everyday example and one safety application for each.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Both primary categories (Contact vs Non-Contact) contain the correct 5 force types.
  2. Each force type includes a valid daily life illustration.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a graphic organizer with pre-labelled root nodes and blank leaf cards.
- **Extension Challenge:** Add atmospheric pressure to the diagram and justify whether air pressure requires contact with surfaces.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, Summary & Exercises, p. 141.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Ready for peer-review rubrics evaluating neatness, taxonomy hierarchy, and scientific vocabulary.

### Sub-Competency 2: Net Force and Directional Interactions (SCI-8-FORCE-C2)

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-2.1
- **Title:** Simulate tug-of-war vector combinations on a wooden block.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Wooden duster/pencil box, string, 2 spring balances or rubber bands)
- **Skill Type:** Procedural Skill & Mathematical Reasoning
- **Assessment Mode:** Performance Task & Worksheet
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Tie two strings to opposite sides of a pencil box. Pull in the same direction with two partners, then pull in opposite directions with equal force, and finally with unequal force. Measure displacement direction and calculate the net force.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. States that forces in the same direction add together ($F_{net} = F_1 + F_2$).
  2. States that forces in opposite directions subtract ($F_{net} = |F_1 - F_2|$).
  3. Explains why equal opposing forces cause zero displacement ($F_{net} = 0$).
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a visual number line where arrows pointing right are positive (+N) and arrows pointing left are negative (-N).
- **Extension Challenge:** Calculate net force when three students pull: two pulling left with 15 N and 10 N, and one pulling right with 30 N.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, Section 11.3 ("Forces applied on an object in the same direction add"), pp. 130–131.
- **Reviewer Notes:** If spring balances are unavailable, calibrated elastic bands with millimeter ruler extension can be substituted.

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-2.2
- **Title:** Calculate net resultant force in 5 illustrated engineering scenarios.
- **Time Estimate:** 20 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Worksheet / Blackboard)
- **Skill Type:** Analytical Thinking & Calculation
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Response
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Solve 5 visual vector scenarios: (1) Two porters pushing a stalled cart in the same direction, (2) Tug of war at equilibrium, (3) A boat rowed upstream against river current, (4) A rocket thrust opposing gravity, (5) A cyclist pedaling against headwind.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Calculates exact numerical net force with correct units (Newtons).
  2. Explicitly specifies direction of motion for each scenario.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide step-by-step formula template: `Net Force = Forward Force - Opposing Force`.
- **Extension Challenge:** Formulate what happens to an object's speed if the net force drops to zero while it is already moving at 20 km/h in frictionless space.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, p. 131; NCF-SE Middle Stage Learning Outcome SCI-8.2.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Introduces the concept of balanced vs unbalanced forces without requiring formal vector trigonometry.

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-2.3
- **Title:** Design a balanced force scale using an improvised clothes hanger.
- **Time Estimate:** 35 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Plastic clothes hanger, twine, two paper cups, coins/erasers for weights)
- **Skill Type:** Collaboration & Practical Inquiry
- **Assessment Mode:** Performance Demonstration
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Build a two-pan balance beam from a hanger. Balance 5 identical coins on each side, then perturb by removing one coin from the left pan. Record observations of torque, unbalanced force, and restoration of equilibrium.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Successfully calibrates hanger to a horizontal equilibrium point.
  2. Explains the transition from balanced state to unbalanced state upon weight disparity.
- **Scaffold Support:** Step-by-step pictorial construction card.
- **Extension Challenge:** Calculate the mass ratio required to balance objects placed at different distances from the center pivot.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE Experiential Learning Pedagogy Manual, Activity 11.4; NCERT Class 8 Science, p. 131.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Low-cost STEM maker activity directly applicable in resource-constrained schools.

### Sub-Competency 3: Effects of Force on Motion and Shape (SCI-8-FORCE-C3)

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-3.1
- **Title:** Investigate changes in speed and direction of a rolling ball.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Tennis/rubber ball, wooden ruler, flat desk)
- **Skill Type:** Procedural & Experimental Observation
- **Assessment Mode:** Performance Task / Lab Log
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Roll a ball on a smooth desk. Apply an additional push in the direction of motion, then place an inclined ruler to deflect it sideways, and finally place a palm in front of the ball. Document what happened to (a) speed, (b) direction, (c) resting state.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Records that force in direction of motion increases speed.
  2. Records that force opposing motion decreases speed or stops object.
  3. Records that force applied at an angle changes direction of motion.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a 3-row structured observation log with checkboxes for (Speed increased / Speed decreased / Direction changed).
- **Extension Challenge:** Connect this observation to how a cricket fielder slows a boundary shot by pulling hands backward during a catch.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, Section 11.4 ("A force can change the state of motion"), pp. 132–134.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Can be conducted in groups of 4 students; requires zero fragile equipment.

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-3.2
- **Title:** Test deformation in 4 common materials under compressive and tensile forces.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Kneaded dough or clay, rubber band, sponge/foam, inflated balloon)
- **Skill Type:** Scientific Inquiry
- **Assessment Mode:** Observation Table & Written Response
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Apply manual force to: (1) pressing dough with a chapati roller, (2) stretching a rubber band, (3) pressing an eraser/sponge, (4) gently squeezing an inflated balloon. Record whether force caused a change in speed, position, or temporary/permanent shape.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Formulates the rule that force can change the shape and size of an object without moving its center of mass.
  2. Differentiates temporary elastic deformation (rubber band) from plastic permanent deformation (dough).
- **Scaffold Support:** Teacher provides physical samples of dough and foam to table groups.
- **Extension Challenge:** Explain why vehicle bumpers are engineered to undergo permanent deformation during collisions to absorb impact force.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, Section 11.5 ("A force can change the shape of an object"), Table 11.2, pp. 134–135.
- **Reviewer Notes:** High tactile engagement for diverse sensory learners.

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-3.3
- **Title:** Measure rolling friction across 3 distinct floor surfaces.
- **Time Estimate:** 35 min
- **Resource Level:** Medium (Toy car or marble, inclined ruler ramp, sandpaper/towel, smooth tile floor, measuring tape)
- **Skill Type:** Measurement, Graphing & Inquiry
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Lab Report with Bar Graph
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Release a toy car from the top of an inclined ruler ramp onto: (a) bare smooth tile/glass, (b) plain cardboard/wood, (c) rough cloth or sandpaper. Measure stopping distance across 3 trials and compute averages.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Records stopping distance with metric units across 3 trials per surface.
  2. Plots a comparative bar chart showing stopping distance inversely proportional to surface roughness.
  3. Deduces that friction is a contact force opposing relative motion.
- **Scaffold Support:** Pre-printed coordinate grid for bar graphing with labeled axes.
- **Extension Challenge:** Propose how Formula 1 car tires balance friction for grip vs. rolling resistance for speed.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, p. 135 & Ch. 12 (*Friction*), Section 12.1–12.2.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Directly connects Chapter 11 (Force) to Chapter 12 (Friction).

### Sub-Competency 4: Real-World Applications & Safety Engineering (SCI-8-FORCE-C4)

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-4.1
- **Title:** Evaluate school zone road safety using force and braking distance principles.
- **Time Estimate:** 30 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Case study sheet, calculator or pencil)
- **Skill Type:** Critical Thinking & Real-World Application
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Case Study Analysis
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Analyze a road safety case study: A school van traveling at 40 km/h requires 16 meters to stop on dry asphalt, but 28 meters on wet monsoon roads. Students compute the impact of reduced friction on stopping distance and propose 3 engineering interventions for the school gate zone.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Explains why water film reduces tire-road contact friction.
  2. Explains the relationship between vehicle momentum (force) and braking distance.
  3. Recommends at least 2 practical safety measures (e.g., rumble strips, 20 km/h speed limit, textured bitumen).
- **Scaffold Support:** Visual flowchart connecting Speed -> Braking Force -> Stopping Distance.
- **Extension Challenge:** Draft an actionable 3-point petition to the local Municipal Corporation for pedestrian speed calming.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 12, pp. 147–149; NCF-SE Interdisciplinary Learning Benchmark 3.4.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Directly feeds into the Multidisciplinary Project Blueprint (Safe School Zone).

#### Task ID: SCI-8-FORCE-4.2
- **Title:** Dissect sports equipment mechanics (Cricket, Badminton, Football).
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Sports gear or illustrated diagram)
- **Skill Type:** Communication & Analytical Thinking
- **Assessment Mode:** Oral Presentation / Flashcard Display
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Select one sport (Cricket bat hitting ball, Football goalkeeper diving to stop shot, Badminton racquet smashing shuttlecock). Create an annotated poster identifying: (1) Contact force applied, (2) Direction of incoming vs. outgoing motion, (3) Opposing frictional or air resistance forces.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Accurately labels at least 3 distinct force vectors on the chosen sports action.
  2. Explains how athletes maximize or minimize force for performance.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a skeleton template diagram of a cricket batsman with arrow prompts.
- **Extension Challenge:** Explain why badminton shuttlecocks have feather skirts from an aerodynamic drag force perspective.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 11, p. 133, Fig 11.4.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Highly engaging for kinesthetic and sports-inclined students.
