# Grade 8 Interdisciplinary: Resources, Development & Sustainability
## Task-Based Learning (TBL) Competency Decomposition

**Curricular Subject:** Interdisciplinary (Social Science: Geography + Science: Environmental Science & Physics)  
**Curriculum Document:** NCERT Grade 8 Geography (Resources and Development, Chapter 1: *Resources*) + NCERT Grade 8 Science (Chapter 5: *Coal and Petroleum* & Chapter 18: *Pollution of Air and Water*); NCF-SE 2023 Curricular Goal C-5.1 & C-5.2 (Environmental Sustainability, Resource Stewardship & Systems Thinking)  
**Competency Statement:** *"Analyze the interdependent relationship between natural/human resources and socio-economic development, evaluate the ecological consequences of over-extraction, and formulate sustainable conservation strategies using the 3R framework and circular economy models."*  
**Track Alignment:** Track A (Interdisciplinary Core Curriculum)  
**Target Grade Band:** Middle Stage (Grade 8, Ages 13–14)  
**Multilingual Key Terms:**  
- **Resources:** Hindi: संसाधन (*Sansaadhan*); Marathi: संसाधने (*Sansaadhane*); Kannada: ಸಂಪನ್ಮೂಲಗಳು (*Sampanmoolagalu*); Tamil: வளங்கள் (*Valangal*)  
- **Sustainable Development:** Hindi: सतत विकास (*Satat Vikaas*); Marathi: शाश्वत विकास (*Shaashwat Vikaas*); Kannada: ಸುಸ್ಥಿರ ಅಭಿವೃದ್ಧಿ (*Susthira Abhivrudhi*); Tamil: நிலையான வளர்ச்சி (*Nilaiyaana Valarchi*)  
- **Conservation:** Hindi: संरक्षण (*Sanrakshan*); Marathi: संवर्धन / संरक्षण (*Samvardhan / Sanrakshan*); Kannada: ಸಂರಕ್ಷಣೆ (*Samrakshane*); Tamil: பாதுகாப்பு / பேணுகை (*Paadhukaappu / Penukai*)  
- **Exhaustibility:** Hindi: समाप्यता (*Samaapyata*); Marathi: संपणारे / अक्षय (*Sampnaare / Akshay*); Kannada: ತೀರುವಿಕೆ (*Teeruvike*); Tamil: தீர்ந்துபோகும் தன்மை (*Theerndhupogum Thanmai*)

## Sub-Competency Architecture

- **Sub-Competency 1 (INT-8-RESDV-C1):** Classify resources into natural, human-made, and human capital based on origin, renewability, levels of development, and distribution.
- **Sub-Competency 2 (INT-8-RESDV-C2):** Investigate the ecological footprint, resource depletion crises, and societal inequalities caused by unsustainable exploitation (groundwater, fossil fuels, mineral mining).
- **Sub-Competency 3 (INT-8-RESDV-C3):** Model the scientific and economic principles of Sustainable Development: balancing current human needs with conservation for future generations without degrading the biosphere.
- **Sub-Competency 4 (INT-8-RESDV-C4):** Execute an authentic school/community resource audit and design an actionable Conservation & Circular Economy Masterplan (Water, Energy, Waste).

## Detailed Task Catalog

### Sub-Competency 1: Taxonomy & Value of Resources (INT-8-RESDV-C1)

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-1.1
- **Title:** Map a Classroom Item from Raw Earth to Value-Added Product.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Classroom pencil, eraser, notebook, desk)
- **Skill Type:** Systems Thinking & Value Chain Analysis
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Flowchart / Pair Share
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Select 1 ordinary classroom object (e.g., a wooden pencil or an aluminium compass box). Trace its lifecycle across 4 evolutionary stages: (1) Natural resource origin (graphite mine, timber forest, bauxite ore), (2) Human capital input (miners, chemical engineers, designers), (3) Technology and machinery (smelting, extrusion, lathe machinery), (4) Finished utility product. Write a brief explaining how human skill transforms natural substances into resources.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Identifies the primary raw natural material and its geographical extraction origin.
  2. Demonstrates the catalytic role of technology and human labor in transforming natural gifts into economic resources.
  3. Cites the NCERT principle: "Utility or usability is what makes an object or substance a resource."
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a 4-box sequential flowchart template with guiding arrows and prompts.
- **Extension Challenge:** Calculate how the economic value increases at each transformation step from 1 kg of raw iron ore (₹5) to steel rods (₹60) to surgical scalpels (₹5,000).
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Geography, Ch. 1 (*Resources*), Section "Types of Resources", pp. 1–3.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Eliminates the misconception that resources are static gifts; emphasizes human agency and skill.

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-1.2
- **Title:** Classify 12 resources by Origin, Renewability, and Development Stage.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Worksheet / Sorting Matrix)
- **Skill Type:** Conceptual Classification & Data Sorting
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Classification Matrix
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Students sort 12 diverse resources (Solar energy, Crude petroleum, Wind, Thorium in Kerala sands, Iron ore, High-speed wind mills in Tamil Nadu, Clean air, Coal in Damodar Valley, Forest timber, Groundwater, Deep ocean manganese nodules, Human software engineers) across a comprehensive 4-dimensional matrix:
  - By Origin: Biotic vs. Abiotic
  - By Exhaustibility: Renewable vs. Non-renewable
  - By Ownership: Individual, Community, National, International
  - By Status of Development: Potential, Developed, Stock, Reserve
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. 100% accurate classification across renewability and origin.
  2. Correctly differentiates Potential resources (Thorium in monazite sands not yet fully utilized) from Developed resources (coal mines in current commercial production).
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a reference definitions card detailing the 4 stages of resource development.
- **Extension Challenge:** Formulate an argument on whether groundwater in Punjab is currently behaving as a renewable or non-renewable resource due to extraction rates exceeding natural recharge.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Geography, Ch. 1, pp. 2–4; NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 5 (*Coal and Petroleum*), p. 56.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Integrates Geography terminology with Science physical laws of conservation of matter.

### Sub-Competency 2: Resource Depletion & Ecological Footprint (INT-8-RESDV-C2)

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-2.1
- **Title:** Calculate Personal Daily Water Footprint and Compare with National Averages.
- **Time Estimate:** 30 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Water audit worksheet, pencil)
- **Skill Type:** Quantitative Literacy & Environmental Awareness
- **Assessment Mode:** Data Log & Written Reflection
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Students log their direct water consumption over 24 hours: (1) Drinking (litres), (2) Brushing/bathing (bucket vs. shower), (3) Toilet flushing, (4) Dishwashing and laundry share. Calculate total litres consumed per person per day. Compare with the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) recommended urban norm of 135 litres per capita per day (LPCD) and rural norm of 55 LPCD.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Accurately estimates daily personal volumetric water consumption with reasonable metric units.
  2. Computes the percentage variance above or below the national benchmark.
  3. Identifies the single largest consumption activity and proposes 2 concrete reduction adjustments.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a standard reference chart of bucket volumes (1 bucket = 15–20 litres, running tap = 6 litres/minute).
- **Extension Challenge:** Investigate the concept of "Virtual Water" (embedded water in goods: e.g., 2,500 litres of water to produce 1 cotton shirt or 1 kg of rice) and compute the virtual water in their school lunch.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Geography, Ch. 2, Table "Water Availability and Use"; Central Water Commission (CWC) Guidelines.
- **Reviewer Notes:** High personal relevance; prompts behavioral shifts within families.

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-2.2
- **Title:** Simulate the "Tragedy of the Commons" using a Fish Pond / Grazing Ground Game.
- **Time Estimate:** 35 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Bowl of dry beans/pebbles, timer, paper cups)
- **Skill Type:** Experimental Inquiry & Game Theory
- **Assessment Mode:** Performance Observation & Group Debrief
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** In groups of 4, students act as competing fishing boats on a shared lake containing 16 beans (fish). In Round 1, each student can harvest as many fish as they want in 10 seconds. Surviving fish double at the end of the round (reproduction). In 95% of trials, students overharvest in Round 1 and drive the lake to extinction. In Round 2, groups establish cooperative quotas to maximize sustainable harvest over 5 continuous rounds.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Documents the outcome of unregulated self-interest (resource collapse).
  2. Formulates sustainable community harvesting rules (limits per person per round to allow natural stock replenishment).
  3. Connects the game directly to commercial ocean overfishing, deforestation, and groundwater table collapse in India.
- **Scaffold Support:** Structured recording ledger for fish counts at start, harvest, and reproduction across rounds.
- **Extension Challenge:** Write a 1-page synthesis connecting the simulation to Elinor Ostrom's Nobel Prize-winning principles for community management of common property resources.
- **Source Citation:** NCF-SE 2023 Cross-Cutting Environmental Education Guidelines; Garrett Hardin's *Tragedy of the Commons* (adapted for middle school).
- **Reviewer Notes:** Experiential learning activity with intense emotional and cognitive impact.

### Sub-Competency 3: Principles of Sustainable Development (INT-8-RESDV-C3)

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-3.1
- **Title:** Deconstruct the 6 Principles of Sustainable Development into Action Statements.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Worksheet / Poster paper)
- **Skill Type:** Conceptual Synthesis & Ethical Reasoning
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Explanatory Poster
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Study the 6 official Principles of Sustainable Development listed in NCERT Geography:
  1. Respect and care for all forms of life.
  2. Improve the quality of human life.
  3. Conserve the earth's vitality and diversity.
  4. Minimise the depletion of natural resources.
  5. Change personal attitude and practices toward the environment.
  6. Enable communities to care for their own environment.  
  For each principle, write an observable "I Will..." pledge and a concrete school policy proposal.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Accurately interprets all 6 principles in plain, student-friendly language.
  2. Distinguishes between economic growth (GDP expansion) and sustainable development (equitable quality of life within ecological planetary boundaries).
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide sentence starters: "To respect all life, our school can... To conserve diversity, we will...".
- **Extension Challenge:** Analyze the Brundtland Commission (1987) definition: *"Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."* Write a critical essay on inter-generational equity.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Geography, Ch. 1, Box "Some Principles of Sustainable Development", p. 5.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Directly anchors ethical and moral responsibility in curriculum standards.

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-3.2
- **Title:** Model a Circular Economy 3R (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle) Waste Hierarchy.
- **Time Estimate:** 30 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Cardboard, markers, clean packaging waste samples)
- **Skill Type:** Design Thinking & Material Science
- **Assessment Mode:** Physical Model / Diagrammatic Poster
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Design an inverted pyramid diagram illustrating the Waste Management Hierarchy: (1) Refuse / Prevent (Top Priority), (2) Reduce consumption, (3) Reuse containers/materials, (4) Recycle through industrial processing, (5) Recover energy (biogas/compost), (6) Landfill Disposal (Last Resort). Map 5 common school waste streams (single-use plastic pens, mid-day meal organic scrap, scrap notebook paper, broken wooden furniture, dry cell batteries) to their highest sustainable tier.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Clearly orders the hierarchy from most preferable (Prevention) to least preferable (Landfill).
  2. Correctly explains why "Reduce" is environmentally superior to "Recycle" (recycling requires energy, water, and generates emissions).
  3. Assigns all 5 school waste streams to appropriate processing methods.
- **Scaffold Support:** Color-coded pyramid diagram template (Green at top $\rightarrow$ Red at bottom).
- **Extension Challenge:** Design an eco-friendly alternative for the disposable plastic ballpoint pen using bamboo, seed paper, or refillable stainless steel ink cartridges.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 18 (*Pollution of Air and Water*), p. 248; Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) Solid Waste Management Rules 2016.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Directly actionable in school sanitation drives and Swachh Bharat Vidyalaya initiatives.

### Sub-Competency 4: School Resource Audit & Action Plan (INT-8-RESDV-C4)

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-4.1
- **Title:** Conduct a Campus Energy & Lighting Efficiency Walkthrough Audit.
- **Time Estimate:** 40 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Clipboard, audit checklist, measuring tape or step counter)
- **Skill Type:** Hands-on Field Investigation & Data Collection
- **Assessment Mode:** Campus Audit Report & Presentation
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** In teams of 4, students inspect 3 designated school zones (Classrooms, Corridors, Staffroom/Library). The team audits: (1) Number of lights/fans left running in empty rooms, (2) Light fixture types (Incandescent vs. Fluorescent Tube vs. LED), (3) Natural daylight availability vs. artificial lighting usage, (4) Estimate daily kilowatt-hour (kWh) electricity consumption and calculate the potential monetary and carbon savings of switching 100% to LEDs and daylight harvesting.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Records physical count of electrical appliances across all inspected rooms.
  2. Calculates approximate wattage and daily energy consumption ($Power\ (\text{kW}) \times Hours$).
  3. Formulates a 3-point actionable Energy Conservation Protocol for the school principal.
- **Scaffold Support:** Pre-printed Energy Audit Form with appliance wattage cheat sheet (LED bulb = 9W, Tube light = 40W, Ceiling fan = 75W).
- **Extension Challenge:** Calculate how many solar photovoltaic panels (350W each) would be needed on the school rooftop to make the school net-zero electricity during daytime hours.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Science, Ch. 5, p. 57; Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) School Energy Club Guidelines.
- **Reviewer Notes:** High experiential value; empowers students as real stewards of school infrastructure.

#### Task ID: INT-8-RESDV-4.2
- **Title:** Design a Rainwater Harvesting System for the School Rooftop.
- **Time Estimate:** 35 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Ruler, grid paper, sketch pens)
- **Skill Type:** Interdisciplinary Engineering Design & Calculation
- **Assessment Mode:** Architectural Blueprint & Written Plan
- **Track Alignment:** Track A
- **Task Description:** Combine Mathematics, Geography, and Science to design an on-site rooftop rainwater harvesting plan:
  1. Measure or estimate school roof catchment area ($A = \text{Length} \times \text{Breadth}$ in square meters).
  2. Use local annual rainfall data (e.g., 800 mm in Delhi, 2,200 mm in Mumbai).
  3. Calculate potential annual water yield: $\text{Harvested Volume} = \text{Roof Area}\ (\text{m}^2) \times \text{Rainfall}\ (\text{m}) \times \text{Runoff Coefficient}\ (0.85)$.
  4. Draw a schematic blueprint showing roof catchments, downpipes, mesh filter chamber, and recharge pit / storage sump.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Mathematically calculates potential annual harvestable water volume in cubic meters and litres.
  2. Blueprints all 4 essential engineering components of rooftop harvesting (Catchment, Conduit, Filter, Storage/Recharge).
  3. Explains how recharging local aquifers raises the groundwater table.
- **Scaffold Support:** Formula guide card with pre-calculated numerical examples for a $100\text{ m}^2$ roof.
- **Extension Challenge:** Calculate how many months of school toilet flushing or garden irrigation could be supported solely by the harvested rainwater reservoir.
- **Source Citation:** NCERT Class 8 Geography, Ch. 2, Section "Rainwater Harvesting", p. 16; Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) Manual.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Integrates Math (Area, Volume, Units) + Science (Hydrology, Filtration) + Geography (Rainfall, Aquifers).
