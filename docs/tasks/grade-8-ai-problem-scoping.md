# Grade 8 Artificial Intelligence: Problem Scoping & Data Requirements
## Task-Based Learning (TBL) Competency Decomposition

**Curricular Subject:** Artificial Intelligence / Skill Education (Middle Stage, Grade 8)  
**Curriculum Document:** CBSE AI Skill Module (Subject Code 901A / Middle School AI Framework), Unit 1: *Problem Scoping*; NCF-SE 2023 Curricular Goal C-7.1 & C-7.2 (Computational Thinking & Emerging Technologies)

**Track Alignment:** Track B (Optional Skill Module) / Track C (Vocational & Skill Elective)  
**Target Grade Band:** Middle Stage (Grade 8, Ages 13–14)

## Sub-Competency Architecture

- **Sub-Competency 1 (AI-8-SCOPE-C1):** Deconstruct an authentic school or community challenge using the standard 4Ws Problem Canvas (Who, What, Where, Why).
- **Sub-Competency 2 (AI-8-SCOPE-C2):** Distinguish between rule-based computational solutions (deterministic logic) and learning-based AI solutions (pattern recognition).
- **Sub-Competency 3 (AI-8-SCOPE-C3):** Specify data requirements, modalities (text, numeric, image, audio), volume, and acquisition channels for an AI model.
- **Sub-Competency 4 (AI-8-SCOPE-C4):** Evaluate data authenticity, representative balance, privacy considerations, and ethical bias in training datasets.

## Detailed Task Catalog

### Sub-Competency 1: The 4Ws Problem Canvas Framework (AI-8-SCOPE-C1)

#### Task ID: AI-8-SCOPE-1.1
- **Title:** Complete a 4Ws Canvas for a School Canteen Food Waste Challenge.
- **Time Estimate:** 30 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Printed 4Ws canvas sheet or blackboard)
- **Skill Type:** Critical Thinking & Systems Analysis
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Problem Canvas
- **Track Alignment:** Track B / Track C
- **Task Description:** Students investigate daily food wastage in the school canteen. They populate all 4 quadrants of the CBSE 4Ws Canvas:
  1. **Who:** Who are the stakeholders facing the problem? (Canteen staff, students, school administration).
  2. **What:** What is the nature and evidence of the problem? (25 kg cooked rice wasted daily due to inaccurate attendance forecasting).
  3. **Where:** In what context and location does the problem arise? (School dining hall during lunch hour).
  4. **Why:** Why will solving it create value? (Reduces cost, conserves food resources, minimizes landfill methane).
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Identifies at least 2 distinct stakeholder groups in the "Who" quadrant.
  2. Articulates measurable quantitative evidence in the "What" quadrant.
  3. Formulates a clear value proposition in the "Why" quadrant.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a guided question cheat sheet for each "W" quadrant.
- **Extension Challenge:** Formulate an official "Problem Statement Template" in one cohesive sentence: *"Our [stakeholders] have a problem that [issue] when [context]. An ideal solution would [benefit]."*
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Curriculum Class 8/9, Unit 1: Problem Scoping, pp. 12–15; CBSE Facilitator Handbook for AI.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Low resource requirement; provides a structured methodology applicable across all technical domains.

- **Skill Type:** Collaboration & Community Scoping
- **Assessment Mode:** Peer Review / Group Presentation

Identify stakeholders (walking students, auto-rickshaw drivers, school security guards, parents), describe near-miss traffic conflicts, and write the core problem statement.
- **Success Criteria:**

  2. Defines how smart traffic monitoring or automated speed alerting could intervene.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a sample completed 4Ws canvas for a library book tracking issue as a reference exemplar.
- **Extension Challenge:** Propose how computer vision cameras could detect whether oncoming vehicles obey the 20 km/h speed threshold.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Skill Module 901A, Unit 1; NCF-SE Middle Stage Goal C-7.2.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Directly integrates into the Multidisciplinary Project Blueprint (Safe School Zone).

### Sub-Competency 2: Rule-Based vs. AI Learning Decisions (AI-8-SCOPE-C2)

#### Task ID: AI-8-SCOPE-2.1
- **Title:** Classify 6 automation scenarios as Rule-Based (If-Else) vs. Machine Learning (AI).
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Classroom cards or worksheet)
- **Skill Type:** Critical Thinking & Architectural Distinction
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Matrix / Justification
- **Track Alignment:** Track B / Track C
- **Task Description:** Analyze 6 real-world technical tasks:
  1. Automatic bell ringing at 8:30 AM based on system clock.
  2. Identifying ripe vs. raw mangoes from smartphone photos.
  3. Calculating student percentage by summing exam marks.
  4. Spam email filter detecting phishing messages based on linguistic patterns.
  5. ATM machine dispensing cash when valid PIN is keyed in.
  6. Voice assistant recognizing spoken regional accents (Hindi, Tamil, Marathi).  
  Classify each as Rule-Based or AI-Based and write the technical justification.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Correctly classifies all 6 scenarios into Rule-Based vs. AI.
  2. Explains that Rule-Based systems follow developer-coded static logic (`IF condition THEN action`), whereas AI systems learn implicit mathematical patterns from data examples.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a 2-question decision tree: "Can a programmer write an exact formula for all possible inputs? If yes $\rightarrow$ Rule-based. If inputs have infinite fuzzy variations $\rightarrow$ AI."
- **Extension Challenge:** Design an `IF/ELSE` pseudocode block for the school bell, then explain why the same `IF/ELSE` logic fails when trying to recognize handwritten student signatures.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Facilitator Manual Class 8, Unit 2: "AI Project Cycle: Rule-Based vs Learning-Based Approaches", pp. 24–26.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Foundational conceptual boundary; prevents students from confusing basic scripting with genuine AI.

#### Task ID: AI-8-SCOPE-2.2
- **Title:** Construct an AI Viability Checklist for a Proposed Idea.
- **Time Estimate:** 20 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Rubric sheet)
- **Skill Type:** Evaluative Judgment & Product Thinking
- **Assessment Mode:** Peer Review / Written Checklist
- **Track Alignment:** Track B / Track C
- **Task Description:** Students take an idea (e.g., "An app that automatically grades student essays" or "A solar tracker that turns towards the sun") and evaluate it against 4 AI Viability Criteria: (1) Does it require complex pattern recognition? (2) Is sufficient training data accessible? (3) Does it tolerate occasional probabilistic error? (4) Is a simple rule-based switch cheaper and more reliable?
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Applies all 4 checklist criteria objectively.
  2. Delivers a reasoned final verdict: "Build with AI" or "Solve with Rules/Sensors".
- **Scaffold Support:** Pre-printed 1-to-5 Likert scale scorecards for each criterion.
- **Extension Challenge:** Estimate the financial and computational cost disparity between an analog photodiode solar tracker versus a computer vision neural network camera.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Skill Module 901A; NCF-SE Middle Stage Technological Literacy Goal C-7.1.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Instills prudent engineering discipline: do not over-engineer with AI when simple logic suffices.

### Sub-Competency 3: Data Requirements & Modality Scoping (AI-8-SCOPE-C3)

#### Task ID: AI-8-SCOPE-3.1
- **Title:** Specify Data Requirements for a School Plant Disease Identifier.
- **Time Estimate:** 30 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Sample leaf specimens or photographs, worksheet)
- **Skill Type:** Data Literacy & Feature Identification
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Data Specification Sheet
- **Track Alignment:** Track B / Track C
- **Task Description:** For an AI model that identifies leaf diseases (e.g., rust, powdery mildew on school garden hibiscus or tomato plants), students formulate a Data Specification Table detailing:
  - **Data Modality:** Image (RGB photos).
  - **Target Classes:** Healthy, Rust, Mildew, Pest-damaged.
  - **Sample Volume Needed:** At least 200 images per class.
  - **Capture Variations:** Morning lighting, evening lighting, leaf topside, leaf underside, diverse angles.
  - **Collection Tool:** Smartphone camera / digital camera.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Defines data modality, label classes, and minimum sample quantity.
  2. Specifies environment lighting and angle variations to prevent model overfitting.
  3. Outlines clean labeling methods.
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a sample data specification sheet for a cat vs. dog image classifier.
- **Extension Challenge:** Specify metadata attributes (e.g., timestamp, ambient humidity, leaf age) that could improve prediction accuracy when combined with image data.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Curriculum Class 8/9, Unit 2: "Data Acquisition & Exploration", pp. 30–34.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Can be integrated with Science botany units and school garden activities.

#### Task ID: AI-8-SCOPE-3.2
- **Title:** Classify and audit 4 Primary Data Modalities in School Systems.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Computer or paper flashcards)
- **Skill Type:** Conceptual Understanding & Technical Taxonomy
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Classification Matrix
- **Track Alignment:** Track B / Track C
- **Task Description:** Investigate how modern AI systems process 4 distinct data modalities:
  1. **Numeric / Tabular Data:** Attendance records, examination marks, temperature logs.
  2. **Text / Natural Language:** Student essays, library book summaries, feedback comments.
  3. **Visual / Computer Vision:** CCTV frames, ID card scans, science lab experiment photos.
  4. **Audio / Speech:** Morning assembly announcements, oral reading recordings.  
  Provide one authentic school AI use case and list 2 required features for each modality.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Accurately links data types to computer representations (tables, token strings, pixel arrays, waveforms).
  2. Identifies relevant predictive features for each modality.
- **Scaffold Support:** Graphic organizer showing how computers view pictures as number matrices (pixels 0–255).
- **Extension Challenge:** Explain why training an AI model on video requires exponentially more memory and compute than tabular spreadsheet data.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Facilitator Handbook Class 8, Three Domains of AI (CV, NLP, Data Sciences), pp. 18–21.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Directly teaches the foundational 3 domains of CBSE AI: Computer Vision, Natural Language Processing, and Data Sciences.

### Sub-Competency 4: Data Quality, Bias & Ethical Considerations (AI-8-SCOPE-C4)

#### Task ID: AI-8-SCOPE-4.1
- **Title:** Detect dataset bias in a simulated facial recognition attendance dataset.
- **Time Estimate:** 30 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Sample image set cards or printed demographic breakdown)
- **Skill Type:** Critical Thinking, Ethics & Social Awareness
- **Assessment Mode:** Written Diagnostic Report
- **Track Alignment:** Track B / Track C
- **Task Description:** Students examine a sample training dataset of 500 photos collected for an automated school gate attendance system. The audit reveals: 80% boys, 20% girls; 90% bright sunny photos, 10% cloudy/dark; zero representation of students with spectacles or religious head coverings (turbans, hijabs). Students identify 3 potential model failure modes and write an action plan to balance the dataset.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Identifies demographic and environmental under-representation in the dataset.
  2. Explains the concept of algorithmic bias (*Garbage In, Garbage Out*).
  3. Formulates a concrete sampling re-balancing plan (equal gender split, lighting variations, inclusive accessories).
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide a definition card explaining "Representation Bias" and "Sampling Bias" with visual bar graphs.
- **Extension Challenge:** Formulate an AI Ethics Charter for Indian schools addressing student privacy, biometric consent, and fairness.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Class 8/9, Unit 1: "AI Ethics & Fairness"; NCF-SE 2023 Cross-Cutting Theme: Ethics in Technology.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Essential citizenship competency; aligns technology education with Constitutional values of equality and non-discrimination.

#### Task ID: AI-8-SCOPE-4.2
- **Title:** Conduct a privacy impact assessment for an AI voice assistant in the classroom.
- **Time Estimate:** 25 min
- **Resource Level:** Low (Case study sheet)
- **Skill Type:** Communication & Ethical Deliberation
- **Assessment Mode:** Structured Debate / Written Privacy Checklist
- **Track Alignment:** Track B / Track C
- **Task Description:** Evaluate a proposal to install an AI smart speaker in Grade 8 classrooms to record lectures and generate automatic revision summaries. Teams analyze: (1) Whose voice data is recorded? (2) Where is audio stored (cloud vs. local)? (3) Who has access? (4) What happens if a student says something private? Draft 4 mandatory privacy guidelines.
- **Success Criteria:**
  1. Identifies privacy risks associated with continuous ambient audio recording.
  2. Articulates the necessity of parental and student informed consent.
  3. Formulates practical safeguards (e.g., physical mute switch, no commercial advertising usage, automatic deletion after 30 days).
- **Scaffold Support:** Provide simplified extracts from India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act regarding minors' data.
- **Extension Challenge:** Propose how "Edge AI" (processing on the device without internet upload) addresses privacy risks compared to cloud APIs.
- **Source Citation:** CBSE AI Curriculum Class 8, Module: AI Ethics & Privacy; Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) 2023 Principles.
- **Reviewer Notes:** Highly topical discussion fostering digital citizenship and critical engagement with modern tech.
