## 1. Grade Profile & Developmental Milestones

### 1.1 Stage Classification & Age Span
- **Stage:** Preparatory Stage (Grades 3–5)
- **Target Age:** 9–10 years
- **Pedagogical Philosophy:** Transition from pure play and activity-based discovery to structured experiential learning. Introduction of textbook literacy, formal spatial and quantitative measurement, and systematic inquiry into the natural and social environment.

### 1.2 Key Developmental Milestones
1. **Cognitive (Quantitative & Spatial Reasoning):** Solves 3-to-4-digit arithmetic problems (addition, subtraction, multi-digit multiplication, simple division with remainders); understands standard metric units (kg, g, L, mL, m, cm); interprets simple pictographs and bar charts.
2. **Cognitive (Environmental & Scientific Inquiry):** Distinguishes between natural vs man-made resources; identifies water cycles and community water filtration methods; constructs basic food webs.
3. **Language & Literacy:** Reads multi-paragraph narratives (100–150 words) with expressive intonation; extracts key evidence to justify story interpretations; composes structured paragraphs with clear topic sentences.
4. **Physical & Fine Motor Dexterity:** Handles compasses, rulers, and measuring tapes with millimeter accuracy; executes coordinated team physical games with adherence to rules.
5. **Socio-Emotional & Ethical Habits:** Demonstrates empathy for peers from diverse backgrounds; understands community division of labor; practices civic cleanliness and natural resource conservation without constant prompting.

### 1.3 Bridge & Transition Criteria
- **Bridge from Grade 3 (Entry Competencies):**
  - Fluent 2-digit mental math and column addition/subtraction up to 999.
  - Decoding grade-appropriate texts at 40+ words per minute.
  - Observational familiarity with local plants, animals, and family structures.
- **Readiness Indicators for Grade 5 (Exit Competencies):**
  - Confident handling of 4-digit arithmetic, fractions (halves, quarters, eighths), and perimeter measurement.
  - Systematic tracking of fair-test experiments (variable control in plant growth or evaporation).
  - Independent formulation of inquiry questions for project-based learning.

## 4. Sample Unit & Lesson Exemplar: Grade 4 Mathematics & EVS
**Unit Title:** The Water Caravan: Measurement, Volume, and Community Stewardship  
**Duration:** 3 Weeks (15 Sessions · 45 mins/session)  
**Driving Question:** "How do we measure, balance, and conserve the exact amount of water our classroom and school garden need daily?"  
**Integrated Competencies:** `C-G4-M-02` (Weight & Capacity Conversion), `C-G4-EVS-01` (Water Purity & Conservation), `C-G4-L1-01` (Descriptive Log Composing).

### 4.1 Lesson Sequence
- **Session 1–3: The Weight of Water:** Students balance 1-liter plastic bottles on two-pan scales against 1-kilogram metal weights to discover that 1 liter of pure water weighs approximately 1 kg. Measure half-liters (500g) and quarters (250g).
- **Session 4–7: School Water Audit:** Teams inspect drinking water taps, kitchen sinks, and toilet flush systems. Count leaking drips per minute in a graduated cylinder. Calculate total wasted volume per week.
- **Session 8–11: Design a Low-Cost Sand Filter:** In groups of 4, construct a three-layer gravel, charcoal, and fine-sand water filtration funnel. Compare water clarity before and after (turbidity observation).
- **Session 12–14: Data Visualization & Presentation:** Graph the daily water consumption of the school garden using bar cards. Write a 5-sentence recommendation letter to the School Management Committee (SMC).
- **Session 15: Unit Assessment:** Print-ready worksheet below.

### 4.2 Differentiation & Inclusion Tips
- *Scaffold Support:* Provide pre-marked balance cards and bottle templates with visual fill lines for students developing spatial intuition.
- *Extension Challenge:* Challenge advanced learners to calculate the financial cost of 2,000 liters of wasted water at commercial municipal tariff rates (e.g., ₹15 per 1,000 L).
